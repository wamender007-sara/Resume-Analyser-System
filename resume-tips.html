10 Resume Mistakes That Get Rejected by ATS Systems

Published by ResumeReviewer Career Guidance Team

Modern corporate hiring relies heavily on Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to screen thousands of resumes before a human recruiter ever sees them. In fact, research indicates that over 70% of candidate resumes are filtered out automatically due to parsing errors and unoptimized formatting. Avoid these 10 critical mistakes to ensure your resume navigates algorithmic screening smoothly and lands directly in front of hiring managers.

1. Using Tables, Graphics, and Multi-Column Layouts

Many candidates design complex multi-column resumes or embed skill ratings into graphics and tables to make them visually striking. Unfortunately, standard ATS parsers read documents horizontally, leading to scrambled text, merged sentences, and lost contact details. Stick to clean, single-column linear layouts with standard text hierarchies.

2. Missing Keywords from the Target Job Description

ATS software algorithmically scores your resume against the target job posting by calculating keyword relevance. If the job description specifically asks for "RESTful APIs" and "PostgreSQL", but your resume only vaguely mentions "backend development", the system may rank your candidacy low. Incorporate exact skills and industry terms naturally throughout your experience.

3. Relying on Exotic or Unstandardized Fonts

Fancy scripts, decorative typefaces, and custom downloadable web fonts often fail to embed properly when converted into plain text streams during ATS extraction. This can transform your achievements into illegible symbols. Always use universally supported typography like Arial, Calibri, Helvetica, Times New Roman, or Georgia.

4. Storing Contact Information in Document Headers or Footers

Placing your phone number, email address, and LinkedIn profile inside Microsoft Word or PDF header and footer sections is a common design choice. However, many parsing engines discard header and footer zones entirely to save processing overhead, leaving recruiters unable to contact you even if you match the job.

5. Inconsistent Date Formatting Across Roles

Applicant tracking systems parse employment timelines to calculate your total years of relevant experience. If you mix conflicting formats—such as "05/2023", "June 2023", and "2023 - Present"—the parser may fail to compute your tenure correctly or flag unexplained employment gaps. Adopt a consistent format like "Month Year – Month Year".

6. Overly Creative or Ambiguous File Names

Saving your resume as "Document1.pdf", "Resume_Final_v3_updated.pdf", or an ambiguous nickname looks unprofessional and confuses automated intake pipelines. Use a clean, standardized naming structure that includes your full name and target role, such as "John_Doe_Software_Engineer_Resume.pdf", ensuring it is easily retrievable.

7. Submitting Unsupported or Image-Based File Formats

Exporting your resume as an image-only scanned PDF, PNG, or TIFF prevents optical and digital text extractors from reading any words. Unless specified otherwise, always submit either a clean text-based PDF or a standard DOCX file with selectable, highlightable text.

8. Typos, Grammatical Slips, and Spelling Errors

Unlike human recruiters who might understand what you meant despite a minor typo, ATS algorithms match exact strings. Misspelling critical keywords like "Python" as "Phyton" or "Kubernetes" as "Kubernets" means the algorithm will treat those required skills as completely missing from your resume.

9. Generic Job Titles That Do Not Match the Role

Internal company designations like "Technical Ninja", "Associate II", or "Member of Technical Staff" do not clearly convey your specialization to an algorithm scanning for "Frontend Developer" or "Data Analyst". Align your functional job titles with standard industry terminology to guarantee immediate relevance.

10. Omitting a Dedicated Skills Section

Hiding technical proficiencies and domain tools only within narrative bullet points makes it harder for semantic parsers to catalogue your core competencies. Include a clearly defined, categorised "Skills" section grouped into Programming Languages, Frameworks, and Tools to maximize ATS keyword extraction.
